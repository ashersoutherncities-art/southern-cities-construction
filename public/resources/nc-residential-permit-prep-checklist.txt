NC Residential Permit Prep Checklist
Southern Cities Construction

This free checklist is meant to help owners, investors, and project managers organize a residential permit submission before it reaches the plan-review desk.

1. Verify the property and jurisdiction
- Confirm the exact site address.
- Confirm parcel number / tax ID.
- Confirm the city or county jurisdiction handling the permit.
- Confirm whether zoning, HOA, floodplain, or historic-district issues apply.

2. Define the exact scope of work
- Write a short scope narrative in plain language.
- Identify whether the work is repair, renovation, addition, change of use, or new construction.
- List any structural, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, roofing, window, or layout changes.
- Make sure the scope narrative matches the drawings.

3. Confirm contractor and owner information
- Property owner legal name and contact info.
- Contractor legal business name.
- License information where required.
- Best contact for corrections and inspector communication.

4. Gather the plan set
- Site plan if required.
- Existing and proposed floor plans.
- Framing / structural details if structure is changing.
- Elevations / sections if needed by the jurisdiction.
- MEP layouts if the scope requires them.
- Any manufacturer specs that support special assemblies.

5. Gather supporting documents
- Scope narrative.
- Contractor proposal or cost summary if the jurisdiction asks for valuation support.
- Engineering or sealed drawings if required.
- Energy forms if applicable.
- Owner authorization letter if someone else is filing.
- HOA approvals if applicable.

6. Check common missing items before filing
- Address is consistent on every document.
- Property owner name is consistent on every document.
- Scope narrative matches the plan set.
- All drawing sheets are labeled and readable.
- Revisions are dated.
- Contact email and phone are included.

7. Clarify inspections before work starts
- Know what inspections will be required.
- Know whether trade permits must be pulled separately.
- Know whether temporary power, erosion, or special inspections apply.
- Know who is responsible for scheduling inspections.

8. Pre-submission questions to answer
- What would cause this permit to be rejected immediately?
- What part of the scope is least clear on paper?
- If the reviewer asked one follow-up question, what would it probably be?
- Are we filing a clean first package, or are we already relying on corrections?

9. Final pre-submission review
- Open every file before upload.
- Confirm every file name is clear.
- Confirm all pages are right-side up and legible.
- Confirm there are no draft sheets mixed into the final submission.
- Confirm the permit value and scope are realistic.

10. Submission follow-through
- Save the submission confirmation.
- Track the permit number.
- Track reviewer comments and correction deadlines.
- Keep one running log of all resubmissions and responses.
