Owner Scope-Lock Worksheet
Southern Cities Construction

Use this worksheet before construction starts so scope, allowances, open items, and decision timing are clearer.

Project basics
- Property address:
- Owner name:
- Primary contact:
- Target start date:
- Target completion date:
- Project type:

1. What is actually being done?
- List every room, area, or exterior zone included.
- List any work the owner assumes is included but has not been clearly written anywhere.
- List anything intentionally excluded.

2. Priority list
- What has to be done?
- What should be done if budget allows?
- What can wait until later?

3. Allowances and selections
For each major selection item, write:
- Item category
- Allowance amount
- Who selects it
- Deadline for final selection
Examples:
- Flooring
- Tile
- Cabinets
- Countertops
- Plumbing fixtures
- Lighting fixtures
- Appliances
- Paint level / finish level

4. Open items that create change orders later
- Structural questions still not answered
- Hidden condition risks
- Permit questions still unresolved
- Utility / service questions
- Material lead-time risks
- Finish selections still undecided

5. Budget pressure points
- What part of the budget feels least certain?
- What part of the scope is most likely to expand?
- What part of the project would hurt most if the timeline slips?

6. Decision deadlines
- Final scope lock date:
- Permit submission date:
- Material selection deadlines:
- Contractor / trade award deadlines:
- Lender or draw deadlines:

7. Change-order rules
- What counts as a change?
- Who approves changes?
- How are changes priced and documented?
- How will timeline impact be communicated?

8. Communication expectations
- Who gets updates?
- How often?
- What should trigger an immediate call vs a normal update?
- Where are approvals documented?

9. Before construction starts, confirm
- Scope is written clearly.
- Exclusions are written clearly.
- Allowances are realistic.
- Open items are identified.
- Timeline assumptions are realistic.
- Permit path is clear enough to move.
